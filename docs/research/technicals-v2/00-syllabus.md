# Technicals v2 — the summer-internship syllabus (research note)

_Written 2026-08-28 for Loops 11–18. Question: for a UK penultimate-year ("second-year summer")
investment-banking summer-internship process, what technicals does a candidate actually need, at
what depth, and which questions come up most? Sources are public forums, prep sites and firm-tagged
question banks (list at the end). Frequency claims are those sources' own. The 400Q guide is used
only for section **counts** (`docs/research/400q-taxonomy.md`); none of its text is reproduced._

## 1. Headline findings
1. **The London summer bar is "perfect accounting / DCF / comps, good LBO, basic merger model."**
   A London PE associate's widely-shared WSO guide states it that way and says mastery of a standard
   textbook (Rosenbaum & Pearl) is enough.
2. **Technicals live at the assessment centre, not the HireVue.** Firm-tagged UK banks put almost
   every GS/MS/JPM HireVue question under motivation / competency / commercial awareness; technicals
   are tagged "Interview / AC". Spring-week ACs run lighter; summer and full-time ACs test deeper.
3. **Firm type matters more than the topic list.** GS London summer ACs have been reported as
   almost non-technical; PJT London is "one of the most technical interviews in London" (with
   restructuring); Evercore's very-common question is a *full* LBO walk-through.
4. **LBO is asked at summer level, conceptually.** Full Excel LBOs are FT/associate/PE territory,
   but paper LBOs are reported at SA level at Citi EMEA, Evercore, Moelis, PJT London and HL Rx.
5. **Interviewers depth-test.** Follow-ups are engineered to expose memorised answers.

## 2. Topic × depth (what we teach vs what we only name)

| Topic | `sa-core` — everyone | `sa-stretch` — EB / LevFin / later rounds | `ft-only` — name in the cheat sheet, don't teach |
|---|---|---|---|
| Accounting & links | The three statements; how they link; D&A +£10 **with the tax effect** (the classic fail is forgetting tax); inventory bought on credit; raising debt / buying PP&E with debt; working-capital moves and why an *increase* is a cash outflow; why a profitable company can go bust; which statement matters most and why | PIK interest through the statements; asset sale at a gain; deferred revenue | Detailed deferred tax on write-ups; lease accounting mechanics; NOL valuation |
| EqV vs EV | Definitions; the bridge (cash, debt, prefs, NCI, pensions, IFRS 16 leases); which multiples pair with which value and why; can EV be negative; what raising debt (or cash) does to each | Diluted shares with the treasury-stock method; convertibles; dividends / buybacks and EV vs equity | Exotic bridge items (restricted cash rules, equity-method detail) |
| Valuation | The three methods; **rank them** (which gives the highest and why — precedents carry a control premium); EV/EBITDA vs EV/EBIT vs P/E and when revenue multiples are used; why two similar companies trade at different multiples; how to present a range | SOTP; valuing negative-cash-flow / pre-revenue companies; liquidation value | Sector multiples unless the candidate claims the group (→ lenses) |
| DCF | The five-step walk-through; **UFCF** formula; **WACC** and **CAPM** cost of equity; both **terminal value** methods and the implied-growth / implied-multiple cross-check; which assumptions the value is most sensitive to; unlevered vs levered and why unlevered; why cost of debt < cost of equity and why not 100 % debt; direction of the mid-year convention | Un/relevering beta; "your DCF says £50, the share is £75 — three hypotheses"; changing rates / tax / capex | Stub periods; private-company WACC; size premia |
| M&A | What accretion / dilution is and the P/E (earnings-yield) rule for all-stock deals; cash vs stock vs debt; cost vs revenue synergies (credibility); why companies acquire; goodwill = premium over net assets; talking about a deal (buyer, seller, price, multiple, rationale, your view) | The merger maths with a mix of cash / stock / debt; PPA write-ups and their DTLs | Full merger model; asset vs stock sale (US tax elections) |
| LBO | What an LBO is; what makes a good target; sources & uses; the **three return levers** (deleveraging, EBITDA growth, multiple expansion); IRR / MoM mental maths (2× in 5 y ≈ 15 %, 3× in 5 y ≈ 25 %) | Paper LBO on one page; debt tranches, cash sweep, PIK; covenants | Full debt schedule; dividend recaps |
| Markets & brain teasers | A recent deal with numbers; a market event; simple estimation; the clock-hands angle | Stock pitch | Option pricing (S&T) |
| Restructuring basics (UK extra) | — | At PJT / Lazard / HL / Rothschild: creditor waterfall, fulcrum security, "a company is in distress — its options" | Cram-down mechanics |

Mid-year convention: candidates should know *what* it is and *which way* it moves value, not compute it.

## 3. Stage by stage (UK process)
- **Online tests** — numerical / verbal / SJT; no finance technicals.
- **HireVue** — 3–5 questions, ~30 s prep / 2 min answer; motivation, competency, commercial awareness,
  occasionally "reasons companies pursue M&A" or a deal you follow. Prepare a deal, a market event,
  and "what does an investment bank do".
- **First round / phone screen** (EBs and mid-market mostly) — behavioural plus basics: "how would you
  value a company", "walk me through a DCF".
- **AC / superday** — 2–4 interviews, sometimes a group case (Evercore: hostile-bid case + quant test)
  or an in-house numerical test (Citi). This is where statement links, EV, valuation ranking, DCF and
  accretion / dilution are asked; LevFin seats add a simple LBO test.
- **Spring week → summer conversion** — final-day interviews plus a later AC; spring-week interviews
  are rarely technical, and over-technical spring-weekers can come across badly. The summer AC
  content still applies to the conversion.

## 4. Top-20 summer-level technicals (ranked by number of independent sources listing them)
Tier A — in essentially every source:
1. Walk me through the three financial statements / how do they link?
2. Depreciation goes up by £10 — walk me through the statements (variants: £200; asset bought with debt; PIK interest).
3. Walk me through a DCF (Lazard: "…and derive the WACC").
4. What are the main valuation methods? / How do you value a company?
5. Rank the methods — which gives the highest value and why?
6. Enterprise value vs equity value — and the bridge between them.
7. How do you calculate WACC? What does it represent?
8. How do you calculate the cost of equity? What is beta?
9. Is cost of debt or equity higher, and why not finance with 100 % debt?
10. How do you calculate unlevered free cash flow?
11. What are the two ways to calculate terminal value, and which is preferred?
12. Why use unlevered rather than levered free cash flow?

Tier B — very common, especially at EBs / later rounds:
13. What is accretion / dilution? All-stock deal at P/E 15 buying P/E 10 — accretive or dilutive?
14. Walk me through an LBO at a high level / what makes a good LBO candidate?
15. Cash vs stock vs debt to fund an acquisition — pros and cons?
16. Why might two similar companies trade at different EV/EBITDA multiples?
17. Can EV be negative? What would that imply?
18. How would you value a company with negative cash flows / no revenue?

Tier C — commercial, in most UK rounds:
19. Tell me about a recent deal (buyer, seller, price, multiple, rationale, your view).
20. What's a current event affecting markets / your view on the M&A market?

## 5. Firm type × stage
- **Bulge brackets** (GS, JPM, MS, Barclays, Citi, BofA): fit-heavy; technicals at standard-guide
  level mixed with markets and a deal. GS London SA ACs reported as near non-technical; MS first
  rounds "more technical and demanding"; JPM London one explicit technical interview; Citi London
  case with comps and an in-house numerical test; BofA adds estimation questions.
- **Elite boutiques** (Evercore, PJT, Lazard, PWP, Moelis, Centerview): technicals-first, longer
  superdays, M&A and LBO maths, follow-ups "on every component"; PJT London mixes restructuring in.
- **UK mid-market / independents** (Rothschild, HL, Jefferies, Numis / Peel Hunt, Big 4 CF):
  accounting fundamentals, three methods + ranking, DCF walk-through; HL Rx adds distress basics.
  Evidence for Jefferies / Peel Hunt / Numis is thin.
- **Product groups**: LevFin — a higher share of technicals, simple LBO tests; ECM — conversational,
  30–40 % technical, heavy on market colour.

## 6. Where candidates fail
- Memorised answers exposed by follow-ups ("flawless technical answers, fell apart on a real deal scenario").
- Missing the tax effect in the depreciation question.
- Not asking clarifying questions when information is deliberately withheld.
- Rambling or two-line answers — "if I have to pester you repeatedly to expand, I'll ding you".
- Over-preparing technicals for spring weeks while under-preparing motivation and a deal.
- Not knowing their own CV; technicals grilled off CV experience.
- Knowing formulas but not *direction* (which way EV moves when debt is raised; why precedents > comps).

## 7. How interviewers probe (design input for `follow_ups` and the probe ladder)
- **Ladder:** walk-through → "which assumption is it most sensitive to and why?" → "your number is
  £50, the market says £75 — three hypotheses and how you'd test them".
- **Contextualise:** the same question dropped into a sector (biotech with no revenue; a bank in
  rising rates) — this is what the industry lens exists for.
- **Vary the transaction:** depreciation → PIK interest → asset sale at a gain → PP&E on debt →
  subscription revenue.
- **Push on a number:** "management says cost of equity should be lower because of X — respond".
- **Challenge and redeem:** interviewers challenge a wrong answer and give a chance to recover.
- **Withhold information** and ask for a **view** ("would you have advised it?").
- Safe move: "I'm not sure, but here's how I'd approach it" — reason aloud.

## 8. What this means for the product (decisions carried into Loops 11–18)
- Scope = the `sa-core` column: **35 lessons, 135 core questions**, +38 stretch, +26 lens = **199 questions**
  (per-chapter split in each `1N-*.md`; 2 of the core questions already exist, so 197 new files).
  Not 400. `ft-only` items appear only in each chapter's cheat-sheet "you may hear it" box.
- Every question carries `depth:` and `format:` tags and 2–3 follow-ups that mirror §7.
- Every walkthrough lesson teaches the *variants* (§7 "vary the transaction"), not one scenario.
- Directional questions ("which way does EV move?") get their own `predict` gates and quick-fire pairs.
- "Talking about a deal" becomes a lesson in the M&A chapter with a fill-in template.
